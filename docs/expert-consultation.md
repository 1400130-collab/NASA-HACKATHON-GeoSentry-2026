# Expert consultation summary (draft)

On 13 November 2026 we spoke by phone for about eight minutes with a landslide researcher at a national research institute in Korea.

## Conditions

- The researcher asked to be cited as an expert consultation, without their name.
- They were speaking from memory. This is not the institute's official position, and some points may need checking.
- They asked us to email before using any wording we are unsure about.
- This summary is paraphrased from a machine transcription that has not been checked by ear. It contains no direct quotes.

## What we learned

**How the warning system works**
- The institute works at national level and has no district of its own.
- It runs a forecast and warning system based on soil water content: how much rain has filled the ground.
- A separate landslide hazard map rates each area by geology and terrain, such as steepness.
- The two are combined into a risk level that changes in real time.
- Nothing described measures whether a slope is actually moving.

**How well it did**
- This year's landslide results had not been compiled yet.
- For 2025, most landslides came with heavy rain and the forecasts at the time were high.
- When asked whether a landslide had happened where risk was rated low, the answer appeared to be "not as far as we know". The recording is unclear at this point.

**What happens after a warning**
- The institute and the Korea Forest Service produce the forecast and pass it on.
- An officer at the local government checks the local rainfall and which mountain areas in their district are especially vulnerable.
- A situation assessment meeting decides whether to evacuate.
- The local government then issues a recommendation or order, and can compel evacuation in very serious cases.

## What it means for the project

- The user is the local government officer, not the research institute.
- Our tool fits the step where the officer checks which areas are especially vulnerable.
- "We catch what the system misses" is not a claim we can make. "We add a measurement the system does not use" is.
